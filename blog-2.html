From KNN to SmartKNN - Evolution of a 50-Year-Old Algorithm

A historical and technical look at how KNN evolved from a simple distance-based method into SmartKNN - a metric-learning inspired algorithm optimized for modern tabular ML.

K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) first appeared in literature around 1967. Over 50+ years, it evolved through multiple breakthroughs: KD-Trees, Ball Trees, Approximate Nearest Neighbors (ANN), metric learning, and finally SmartKNN.

This article traces the evolution of KNN - what each stage solved, where limitations remained, and why SmartKNN represents the next major step.

1967 - Original KNN (The Beginning)

The original KNN introduced a simple but powerful idea:

Predict using similar past cases instead of learning an explicit function.

What it solved

  • No training required
  • Intuitive pattern matching
  • Works well on very small datasets

Limitations

  • Brute-force search → extremely slow on large datasets
  • No feature weighting or selection
  • Breaks in high-dimensional space
  • Highly sensitive to noise

1990s - KD Trees (Solving Speed for Low Dimensions)

What it solved

  • Spatial partitioning enabled O(log N) search
  • Huge improvement over brute force
  • Great for ≤ 20 dimensions

Limitations

  • Fails in high-dimensional data
  • No noise handling
  • No metric learning - still raw Euclidean distance

2000s - Ball Trees (Handling Irregular Distributions)

What it solved

  • Better for data not aligned to axes
  • Partitions space via hyperspheres
  • More robust pruning

Limitations

  • Still collapses beyond 30 - 50 dimensions
  • Still uses equal feature weights
  • Sensitive to outliers and scale

2010s - Approximate Nearest Neighbors (ANN)

What it solved

  • Sub-millisecond search at massive scale
  • Great for embeddings, search engines, vector databases
  • Excellent for image, text, and LLM-based vectors

Limitations

  • Not designed for tabular KNN
  • Does not improve distance quality - only search speed
  • Still assumes raw Euclidean geometry
  • No handling of tabular noise or feature semantics

2020s - Metric Learning (Learning Similarity Itself)

What it solved

  • Learnable distance function
  • Weighted feature spaces
  • Siamese networks, triplet loss, contrastive learning

Limitations

  • Heavy training cost
  • Hard to interpret
  • Not ideal for small tabular datasets

2025 - SmartKNN

SmartKNN is the a KNN variant designed specifically for real-world tabular ML.

What SmartKNN solves

  • Feature weighting using MSE, MI, and ExtraTrees scoring
  • Noise suppression through learned weights
  • Stability in high-dimensional data
  • Auto Preprocessing of Numerical features for speed
  • Interpretability via learned weights & neighbors

Limitations of SmartKNN

  • Not ideal for sparse, ultra-high-cardinality categorical features
  • Still memory-based (stores samples or prototypes)

Final Thoughts

Over 50 years, KNN evolved from simple case-based learning into a sophisticated, metric-aware algorithm. SmartKNN is the newest chapter in this story - bringing feature weighting, stability, and modern engineering into a classic method.
